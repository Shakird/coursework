Our story

North Star Bakery opened in the spring of 2011 in a storefront that had been empty for six years. Maren Okafor had been baking out of a home kitchen and selling at the Saturday market on Alder Street, and when the lease on the corner came up she took it with a used deck oven, a secondhand mixer, and a jar of sourdough starter she had been feeding since culinary school.

The first year was thin. Then the neighborhood found us. People started walking over on Saturday mornings, then on weekdays, then sending their in-laws. The 7:20 commuters worked out that if they left the house four minutes earlier they could have a croissant on the platform. Fifteen years on, that same starter is still in the walk-in, still going, and it is in every loaf we sell.

We are still small on purpose. One shop, one oven, one shift. When the shelves are empty we are done for the day, and we would rather run out than bake more than we can bake well.

How we source

Good bread is mostly good flour and enough time. We spend our money on the first and refuse to cut corners on the second.

  • Flour. Stone-ground wheat and rye from Miller’s Mill, forty minutes north of here, delivered weekly. Unbleached, unbromated, never sitting in a warehouse for a season.
  • Butter. Cultured 83% butterfat from a dairy co-op in the next county. It costs us more than commodity butter and it is the reason the croissants taste the way they do.
  • Fruit and eggs. From the growers at the Alder Street market, May through October. In winter we bake with what keeps: apples, pears, and preserves we put up ourselves in the fall.
  • Time. No dough conditioners, no instant-rise shortcuts, no par-baked anything. Our sourdough ferments for 48 hours because that is how long it takes.
  • What we do with leftovers. Yesterday’s croissants become almond croissants. Day-old loaves go to the Riverton community kitchen every Tuesday and Friday evening. Very little leaves here as waste.

The people behind the counter

Maren Okafor, Owner and Head Baker

Maren opened the shop in 2011 and still runs the bread program. She is in the building by 3:45 a.m. most days, shapes nearly every loaf herself, and will happily talk your ear off about hydration percentages if you give her the opening.

Tobias Reyes, Pastry Chef

Tobias joined in 2016 after nine years in hotel kitchens and rebuilt our entire laminated-dough program. The cardamom cinnamon knot is his. He tests one new seasonal danish a month and lets the Saturday regulars vote on whether it stays.

Dana Whitfield, Cake Decorator

Dana has decorated every celebration cake that has left this shop since 2019. She grew up four blocks from here, and there is a decent chance she has already made a cake for someone on your street.

Sam Lindqvist, Counter Lead

Sam opens the front of house, knows roughly two hundred regulars by their order, and keeps the pre-order board straight during the morning rush. If you called about an allergy, Sam is who wrote it down.
